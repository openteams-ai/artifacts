Nebari OSS & Delivery

A current view of everything underway across Nebari OSS (Open Source Solutions) and Delivery. Each card below is referred to as its own 'Deliverable' — spanning our feature roadmap, delivery processes, tooling and environments, and how we manage change.

These aren't sequential steps — they're parallel efforts, each at its own stage, all working toward the same goal: improving our end-to-end product development and project delivery journey.

A note on timelines

No dates or target timescales here — deliberately. These are meaningful changes, and rolling out too many at once risks doing more harm than good. Delivery also relies on people needed for higher-priority work, e.g. Client Projects, so pace is set by that balance rather than a fixed schedule.

Nebari OSS Roadmap

In progress
Building the feature roadmap for FY26 and onwards

An open, up to date view of the Nebari feature roadmap for the rest of this financial year and onwards — what's being decided now, what's queued up next, and what's still open. This tracks the process of building the roadmap, not the roadmap's feature content itself, which will be shared separately once available.

Roadmap steps
Completed In progress Next up Not started
01

Identify backlog candidates

Completed
Capture top-level product requirements from stakeholders
  • Meet with tech leads, product owners and other key stakeholders to capture their top-level requirements for the product
  • Consolidate everything raised into a single list of backlog candidate items
02

Categorise importance of the backlog

Completed
Steering group votes and agrees relative importance
  • The steering group (product owner and tech leads) votes on and discusses each candidate — including whether it's worth doing at all
  • Combine async voting with backlog discussion calls to verbally agree where each item lands:
  • Now — critical, should be done straight away
  • Next — important, should follow shortly after
  • Parked — worth doing, but lower priority for now
  • Dropped — not needed on the roadmap at all
  • Single-project need — belongs to that project, not the core product; raised there directly, and revisited if the same need comes up elsewhere
03

Size & sequence by team

In progress
Bring in the delivery teams to size and sequence their share
  • Identify which team is responsible for delivering each candidate item
  • Hold discussions with each team to prioritise the items relevant to them
  • Assign a rough effort estimate to each item, based on 1 FTE working on it 100% of the time
  • Review the resulting priority order with the wider leads and product owner to agree the final approach
04

Draft the roadmap

Next up
Lay out the roadmap by priority, size and team split
  • Draft the roadmap using the agreed priority order, effort estimate and team split
  • Identify what's already been delivered so far, alongside what remains
  • Decide the roadmap governance approach — review cadence, meeting structure, and how change requests will feed in (immediate review for very high priority items, folded into regular reviews otherwise)
05

Review & align

Not started

  • Circulate the draft roadmap to product owners and tech leads for review and approval
  • Identify any conflicting priorities and capacity gaps/resource concerns
  • Circulate to stakeholders and senior management for awareness and any initial concerns
06

Publish, communicate & maintain

Not started
Make the roadmap visible to the org and community, then keep it current
  • Finalize the roadmap document / visual for sharing
  • Share with the wider org — and the Nebari open-source community where relevant
  • Schedule the agreed review cadence — backlog review sessions and roadmap item progress check-ins — and communicate how the escalation route works for anything that can't wait for its next scheduled review
Note — the feature candidate backlog constantly evolves as new or higher-priority candidates come in. Changes to this roadmap will eventually be handled through the new Feature Request Process once rolled out, feeding into the same review cadence.

A note on delivery

Publishing and communicating the roadmap isn't the end of it — actually delivering the work is an equal, if not more important, action. Some roadmap candidates are already in progress today, under our current ways of working — though how that's approached may evolve as we improve our development lifecycle, tighten our release approach, and refine how we handle delivery of work.

SizeEffortDescription
XS<1 dayA clearly defined, self-contained change — a config tweak, docs fix, or contained bug. Can be picked up and developed (and tested) the same day before sending for CR.
S1–3 daysWell-understood scope with minimal unknowns — a small feature, isolated refactor, or straightforward bug fix. May require a few days of effort to develop and test before sending for CR.
M3–5 daysModerate complexity with some moving parts. May touch multiple areas and require input from others — ideally broken down into subtasks. Will require up to a week of effort for development and testing before sending for CR.
L5–10 daysSignificant scope spanning multiple components or teams. High risk of unknowns mid-build, and likely a more complex solution. Will require up to 2 weeks of effort for development and testing before sending for CR.
XL>10 daysA more complex implementation for a typical single task — too large to track as a single ticket, with high estimation uncertainty and delivery risk. Must be broken down before it can be scheduled, and will likely be planned as multiple issues rather than one to complete the feature.

Work Delivery Lifecycle

In progress
A proposed delivery approach matched to the size and complexity of the work

As mentioned in the note on delivery, identifying roadmap candidates is only the start of the journey. A consistent, efficient delivery approach is essential to delivering that work successfully.

The proposal — categorise all new product/feature work into three delivery workstreams based on size and complexity — Projects, Small Works and Support — each moving through the lifecycle that fits its scale, rather than forcing everything through the same process — while still allowing the flexibility and pragmatism needed to handle work efficiently.

Delivery lifecycle steps
Completed In progress Not started
01

Define the proposed workstreams & lifecycles

Completed
Categorise work by size and complexity, each with its own proposed lifecycle

Proposed the following three delivery workstreams, not yet confirmed, each moving through the lifecycle that fits its scale:

  • Projects — large, highly complex deliverables spanning multiple teams and months, requiring a dedicated Project/Delivery Manager; follows the five-phase PMBOK model proposed for adoption as project delivery best practice. The actual delivery approach may still differ project to project, reflecting the pragmatic approach needed for successful delivery
  • Small Works — smaller changes that may still span multiple products; splits into two paths by size and complexity, from lightweight single-product changes up to larger, more involved multi-product efforts run closer to a Project
  • Support — continuous maintenance, bug fixes and tech debt, with resource(s) allocated to the workstream itself; reactive to priority items and requests, aiming for a fluid, self-managed workstream driven by a prioritised backlog
